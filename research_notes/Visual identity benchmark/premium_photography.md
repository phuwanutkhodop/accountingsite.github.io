# Premium photography and imagery for a Thai luxury-house accounting / tax / advisory firm

Research date: 2026-10-05. Method: web search plus page fetches. Fetches of hingemarketing.com, web.dev, w3.org and photutorial.com were **blocked by the network egress proxy**, so claims attributed to those domains come from search-result snippets or are flagged as unverified recall. Nothing here comes from first-hand inspection of the brand websites; descriptions of how Pictet, Coutts, Rothschild & Co, Slaughter and May and others look are **unverified** unless a source is cited.

## 1. How private banks, elite law firms, advisories and luxury houses use photography

### Takeaway
The best-documented premium pattern comes from luxury houses (Aesop, Loro Piana, Hermès), not from finance. They use material-led still life, diffused natural-feeling light, a restrained earthy palette, tight crops on texture and craft, and hands rather than faces. Among the private banks, the clearest documented case is Lombard Odier. It hired a branding chief from luxury fashion and builds campaigns on nature and concept imagery, not people at desks. Law-firm practice leans towards documentary "in-situ" portraits under strict art direction.

### Cited Findings
- **Lombard Odier** relaunched its whole brand in 2016 ("Rethink Everything") under a Chief Branding Officer, Fabio Mancone, who came from luxury fashion (Giorgio Armani, Ralph Lauren, L'Oréal prestige fragrances). It was the bank's first global advertising campaign and included a website redesign — [Management Today case study](https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/case-study-rebranding-swiss-bank/reputation-matters/article/1455188); [WealthBriefingAsia](https://www.wealthbriefingasia.com/article.php/Lombard-Odier-Unveils-Global-Brand-Campaign)
- Lombard Odier's 2024 campaign "The Regenerative Power of Nature" won an FT Professional Wealth Management award for "compelling imagery and text". Its subject is nature and landscape, not bankers — [Lombard Odier](https://www.lombardodier.com/fr/insights/2024/november/lombard-odier-s-nature-s-regene.html)
- Pictet, Lombard Odier and Darier Hentsch were among the Swiss private banks that began promoting themselves individually from 1997. Mirabaud and Bordier are described as the last "ultra-discreet" private banks to try advertising — [markt-kom](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=107507); [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/economie/mirabaud-bordier-deux-derniers-banquiers-prives-ultradiscrets-se-frottent-publicite)
- **Aesop**: "amber bottles, stone-toned palettes, restrained lighting, and ingredient-led details" form "a visual system that feels premium without losing realism". The photography "allow[s] textures, materials, and formulations to remain the focus" — [Photoroom, 100 best product pages: Aesop](https://www.photoroom.com/100-best-product-pages/beauty/aesop)
- Aesop still life (photographer Beverley Hyde): products set among wooden blocks, metal cylinders and spheres against muted brown backgrounds. Lighting is "diffused, highlighting the textures of the products and the rough surfaces of the props" — [visie.io](https://visie.io/media/beverley-hyde-68227); [visie.io](https://visie.io/media/beverley-hyde-68237)
- **Loro Piana** handbag campaign (photographer Charles Nègre): "softly lit still lifes and close-up compositions emphasizing tactility", with crops on zips, padlocks, stitching and leather grain. "Restrained palette and measured lighting… luxury is communicated through material intelligence rather than excess" — [The Impression](https://theimpression.com/?p=11829021)
- Loro Piana craft imagery: artisan hands lifting and stretching merino fibres to inspect them. Hands and material are the subject; the face is absent — [Rain Magazine](https://rain-mag.com/loro-piana-record-bale-award-2024-a-new-milestone-in-merino-wool-excellence/loro-piana_record-bale-award_merino-wool-fiber-4)
- **Hermès** "Hearts and Crafts" web content uses video and interviews with real craftspeople, from leather-cutters to silk-drawers. It sells the human touch through actual makers, not models — [PurseBlog forum (secondary)](https://forum.purseblog.com/threads/hermes-hearts-and-crafts-video.723378/)
- **Watchmakers**: Rolex imagery relies on extreme macro close-ups of dials, hands and markers — [visie.io Rolex](https://visie.io/media?q=Rolex) (thin source)
- **Law firms**: a typical rebrand includes "soft focus shots of lawyers". In-situ portraits keep "a professional yet warm atmosphere", with art direction that captures lawyers in their own setting while following the brand guidelines — [Law Gazette, "Image making"](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/image-making/34174.article); [Retines, law-firm portrait in Paris](https://retines.fr/en/blog/project/corporate-portrait-for-a-law-firm-in-paris/); [Lawyers Weekly, "Capturing lawyers in the moment"](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/folklaw/8774-capturing-lawyers-in-the-moment)
- Slaughter and May's identity programme was handled by Northbank Design (2001, chosen from a 14-agency pitch). This is a historical fact only; no current photography details were found — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/28-june-2001/northbank-takes-case-for-slaughter-and-may/)

### Inferences
- These brands share a recipe that fits the oxblood / brass / paper identity directly:
  - **Subjects**: materials (paper, leather, brass, stone, teak, linen), objects of the trade (fountain pen, ledger, seal or stamp, archive boxes, a brass desk lamp), architecture details (stairs, colonnades, shadows on plaster), and hands at work.
  - **Faces**: kept for real people (partners) only.
- **Light and palette**: diffused, directional window light with a muted, earthy palette. This lines up naturally with warm paper and oxblood.
- **Restraint is the signal**. Use few images, at large size, with plenty of empty space around them. This matches the brand's principle of "deliberate emptiness".
- Unverified observation (from memory, not checked this session): Pictet, Rothschild & Co and Coutts sites lean on architecture, landscape and editorial portraiture, use little stock, and show almost no "people in meetings" shots. Verify by visiting the sites before quoting this.

### Gaps
- No primary source (brand guideline, agency case study) was found for photography direction at Pictet, Coutts, Rothschild & Co or Slaughter and May. There is also no data on crop ratios or on what share of each page goes to imagery.
- Nothing was found on It's Nice That, Creative Review or Eye on Design about finance-brand photography (not reached within the search budget).

## 2. What reads "stock / generic" and what reads premium

### Takeaway
"Generic" has consistent, well-documented traits:
- artificial, bright, even lighting
- static poses with people smiling at the camera
- no context
- clichéd subjects such as handshakes, men in suits and skylines

Premium imagery is specific, textural and naturally lit, and ideally shows the firm's own people and places.

### Cited Findings
- Stereotyped stock usually has "artificial, bright lighting; unnatural posing with no movement; people smiling and looking directly into the camera; and a lack of context and relevance" — [ExpertPhotography, 8 types of stock photos to avoid](https://expertphotography.com/types-of-stock-photos)
- "The Handshake… does not say anything specific about the business or why you should choose them beyond having a firm grip" — [LockeDown SEO](https://lockedownseo.com/avoiding-stock-photography-cliches/); see also [MotoCMS](https://motocms.com/blog/en/stock-photo-cliches-and-how-to-avoid-them), [Fremont College](https://fremont.edu/cliche-images-to-avoid-on-your-website/)
- Hinge Marketing (a specialist in professional-services marketing) argues that imagery is an underused part of a firm's visual identity. "If the pictures you associate with your firm are little different from those your competitors use, there is more room for confusion — and forgetting." Quoted from a search snippet; the page itself could not be fetched — [Hinge, "Is your imagery damaging your image?"](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/is-your-imagery-damaging-your-image)
- Photographing your own staff "will make you stand out", because customers want to see the real people and workplace — [LockeDown SEO](https://lockedownseo.com/avoiding-stock-photography-cliches/)
- Depositphotos (a stock library) itself publishes guidance on moving past clichés — [Depositphotos blog](https://blog.depositphotos.com/rethinking-photography-cliches-in-the-new-decade.html)

### Inferences
- A **ban list** for this firm: handshakes; smiling teams around a laptop; pointing at charts on screens; calculators with coins or piggy banks; arrows going up; chess pieces; generic glass skylines; any model looking into the camera in a fake office.
- A **premium list**:
  - texture still life (paper grain, ink, leather, brass)
  - natural window light with real shadows
  - documentary portraits of the actual partners
  - quiet architecture (Bangkok shophouse or colonial-era details, teak, terrazzo, plaster)
  - hands doing real work (signing, turning a page, sorting documents)
  - Thai materials (silk, lacquer, celadon, teak) used sparingly
- Test for any image: **could a competitor post this unchanged?** If yes, it is generic.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found showing that real photography converts better than stock for professional services in 2025–26.

## 3. Colour treatment for an oxblood / brass palette, and keeping a library consistent

### Takeaway
There are three workable routes:
1. **A single warm "house grade" on full-colour photos.** This is the most flexible, and the recommended default.
2. **Black-and-white with a warm tint.** Timeless, and works for portraits and archive-style shots.
3. **Duotone in brand colours** (for example near-black red-brown shadows with paper-colour highlights). Very consistent, but strips depth, so it suits hero or background use rather than everything.

Consistency comes from one fixed recipe applied to every new image, not from the photographer's taste on the day.

### Cited Findings
- Brands "develop bespoke color grading techniques to ensure every photograph seamlessly integrates" with the brand. A bespoke grade "creates consistency throughout imagery". Umicore's brand site even has a dedicated colour-grading page — [Umicore brand guidelines: colour grading](https://brand.umicore.com/en/visual-identity/photography/color-grading/)
- Photo filters "allow the use of images from multiple sources while making them appear more similar to one another" — [WGU brand: lifestyle photography](https://www.brand.wgu.edu/lifestyle-photography)
- Duotone "maps image brightness to two colors: a dark color for shadows and a light color for highlights". It works well for brand consistency and for unifying photos from different sources — [IMG.LY duotone docs](https://img.ly/docs/cesdk/js/filters-and-effects/duotone-831fc5/)
- However, "duotone processing can strip the image of depth and meaning" — [GoSpotCheck brand guidelines (photography)](https://brand.gospotcheck.com/photography)
- A photography "style" can be captured as a preset covering "lighting, color grading, shadow characteristics, and composition" and reused across the catalogue — [Nightjar blog](https://nightjar.so/blog/photography-styles-build-consistent-brand-aesthetic-with-ai)

### Inferences
- **Recommended for this site** (opinion, not sourced):
  - **Default grade**: slightly warm white balance, gently lifted and warm-toned shadows (towards the red-brown night colour), reduced saturation especially in blues and greens, mild contrast, and fine grain added to match the paper texture.
  - **Duotone option**: oxblood or near-black shadows with warm paper highlights. Use it only for full-bleed hero or background images, and for low-quality images that need disguising.
  - **Black-and-white option**: for partner portraits if the shoot conditions vary.
- **Build it into the pipeline.** The site already has an in-browser WebP pipeline. A fixed CSS or canvas "house grade" can be applied at upload (warm tone curve, saturation cap, grain overlay), so every future image gets the same treatment whoever uploads it. The duotone could also be done at render time with an SVG `feColorMatrix` filter or a CSS `mix-blend-mode` overlay in brand tokens, which keeps the original files neutral.
- **Night mode**: images on the near-black red-brown background may need lower brightness or a subtle dark overlay so they do not glare.
- **Rules for the library**: one grade, one grain level, one set of crop ratios (for example 4:5 portrait, 3:2 landscape, 21:9 hero), no mixed colour and black-and-white within one section, and every image approved against a small set of reference images.

### Gaps
- No finance-sector brand guideline publicly documenting a duotone or grade recipe was found.

## 4. Portrait photography of partners and staff

### Takeaway
Use documentary-style portraits on location ("in situ"), lit by soft natural or natural-looking light. One photographer and one setup for everyone, re-used when new people join, keeps the set consistent. For a luxury tone, quieter expressions and environmental context beat studio headshots on white.

### Cited Findings
- Law-firm portraiture practice: in-situ shots under tailored art direction that captures lawyers in their own setting while following brand guidelines, for a "professional yet warm atmosphere" — [Retines (Paris law-firm portrait case)](https://retines.fr/en/blog/project/corporate-portrait-for-a-law-firm-in-paris/)
- "Capturing lawyers in the moment", the documentary rather than posed approach — [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/folklaw/8774-capturing-lawyers-in-the-moment)
- Soft-focus lawyer portraits are a common rebrand element — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/image-making/34103.article)
- US benchmark prices for company headshots in 2026: typically **$150–$350 per person**, falling to **$50–$100 per person** for large groups. The headline range is "$75–$800 per employee" — [BetterPic, 2026 pricing](https://www.betterpic.io/blog/company-headshot-photographer-pricing-2026)

### Inferences
- **Consistency rules** (suggested):
  - same location, or a backdrop in the warm paper or stone tone
  - same focal length and camera height (around eye level, 85mm-equivalent)
  - same light direction, a large soft key light from one side as if from a window
  - same crop (for example 4:5, head to mid-torso)
  - same wardrobe guidance (dark, solid, no logos; oxblood or brass accessories allowed)
  - same grade
- Write the setup down (light positions, settings, crop) so a new hire can be shot to match years later.
- Offer pairs: a formal portrait plus a candid "at work" shot (hands on documents, reading) for the team page and articles.
- Avoid: crossed arms, gritted "power" smiles, glass-office bokeh, and the stock-style pose of looking straight into the lens while laughing.

### Gaps
- **Bangkok pricing in THB was not found.** Searches returned only Airbnb Services listings, which are not corporate studios: "personal/corporate branding from $177 per group" and "corporate event photography from $550 per group" — [Airbnb Services TH](https://th.airbnb.com/services/6886371). Ask two or three Bangkok corporate studios for quotes; do not rely on these figures.

## 5. Video backgrounds: premium or gimmick, and performance

### Takeaway
Video reads premium when it is slow, short, silent, near-monochrome and material-focused: for example ink on paper, light moving across stone, pages turning, a city at dawn with little motion. It reads gimmicky when it is fast-cut, shows stock-people b-roll, competes with the headline, or slows the page.

Technical rules:
- `autoplay muted loop playsinline`
- a poster image that looks like the first frame
- small WebM and MP4 files
- lazy-load below-the-fold video
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by showing the poster only

### Cited Findings
- Video must be muted to autoplay reliably. A background video needs `muted` and `playsinline` plus CSS `object-fit` — [Swarmify](https://swarmify.com/blog/how-to-make-an-autoplaying-background-video/); [Reintech](https://reintech.io/blog/creating-fullscreen-video-background-with-css)
- Without a poster, users see blank space before playback. "Always provide a visually similar poster frame" — [ProfileTree, background videos](https://profiletree.com/background-videos/)
- A background video that hurts Largest Contentful Paint (how fast the main content appears) is counterproductive. Use short seamless loops and load video only when its section enters the viewport — [ProfileTree](https://profiletree.com/background-videos/)
- "Autoplaying background video should be gated on `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, or shipped as a static poster image by default". Decorative animation, parallax and autoplay can trigger vestibular distress, migraines and seizures — [specification.website: reduced motion](https://specification.website/spec/accessibility/reduced-motion/)
- web.dev's canonical guidance on lazy-loading video (recalled, **not verified this session** because the fetch was blocked):
  - for autoplaying "GIF-replacement" video, use `autoplay muted loop playsinline`
  - set `preload="none"` with a `poster`
  - lazy-load sources with IntersectionObserver
  - offer WebM before MP4

  — [web.dev: lazy-loading video](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video)

### Inferences
- **Suggested budget** (opinion): hero loop 6–15 seconds; around 1–3 MB for 1080p, and a smaller rendition for mobile; no audio track; grade it with the same house grade as the photos.
- Consider showing only the poster on mobile or slow connections.
- Add a visible pause control: WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) requires one for moving content that runs longer than 5 seconds. This comes from memory and should be checked against W3C, which could not be fetched.
- Sliders and carousels: keep them slow and controlled by the user. Auto-advancing carousels are widely considered weak for usability (not sourced this session).
- For the GitHub Pages pipeline: video files must sit in the repo, and GitHub warns at 50 MB and blocks files over 100 MB (from memory, unverified). Keep clips small, or host them on a CDN.

### Gaps
- No design-press source (It's Nice That and similar) was found judging when video reads luxury and when it reads gimmicky. The aesthetic guidance above is inference from the luxury-house still-life pattern.

## 6. Where to source imagery: stock libraries, Thai imagery, licences, costs, commissioning

### Takeaway
For premium-looking stock, the realistic options are Stocksy (curated, about $15–$125 per image on its standard licence) and Adobe Stock Premium (about $96–$250 per image). Getty Creative is the most expensive at about $150–$499. Unsplash+ (about $7–$20 per month, with a small indemnity) is a budget option. Free Unsplash carries real legal risk because it gives no model releases and no indemnity. Thai landmark images are widely available as royalty-free stock. The strongest option for a differentiated luxury look is still a commissioned Bangkok shoot, though local prices could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Stocksy**:
  - Standard licence by size: Small $15, Medium $30, Large $75, X-Large $125.
  - Standard covers commercial use, unlimited web use and 500K prints.
  - Optional tiers: Team, Enhanced (Standard + $300, which replaced the old Unlimited Print and Product-for-Resale add-ons) and Market Freeze.

  — [Photutorial, Stocksy review](https://photutorial.com/stocksy-review); [Stocksy Ideas](https://www.stocksy.com/ideas/?p=13289)
- **Adobe Stock**: Premium collection assets cost 25 credits (US$249.99) or 50 credits (US$499.99) on demand; another source gives premium images as $96–$149.99. Standard images cost $0.26–$2.99 each on annual plans, or $8–$9.99 on demand — [Lapse of the Shutter](https://www.lapseoftheshutter.com/adobe-stock-pricing/); [Adobe contributor help: Premium price tier](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/Premium-Price-Tier.html). The two price ranges conflict, so check live.
- **Getty Images**: on-demand royalty-free costs about **$150–$499 per image** — [Lapse of the Shutter, Adobe Stock vs Getty](https://www.lapseoftheshutter.com/adobe-stock-vs-getty/)
- **Unsplash+**: $20 per month billed monthly, or $7 per month billed annually. Covers commercial use and includes a **$10,000 indemnity** — [Photutorial, Unsplash review](https://photutorial.com/unsplash-review); [Stock Titan (launch press release)](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/GETY/unsplash-launches-unsplash-a-new-unlimited-subscription-offering-l70i1bfet1fc.html)
- **Free Unsplash licence**:
  - You may not compile images to build a similar or competing service.
  - It gives **no model releases and no indemnification**, so the user takes on the legal risk.
  - Identifiable people may need their own consent.
  - Trademarks in shot are a risk.

  — [Pixsy, Unsplash guide](https://pixsy.com/image-licensing/unsplash-guide); [LicenseOrg, free stock traps](https://licenseorg.com/blog/free-stock-photos-licensing-traps); [Equinux FAQ](https://www.equinux.com/us/faq/1585/What-do-I-need-to-know-before-I-use-Unsplash-photos)
- **Thai imagery**: Getty, iStock, Alamy and imageBROKER sell royalty-free Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew images marked "No release required" or "Property release: No". Some images are editorial-only, so check each one before commercial use — [Getty example](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/photo/temple-of-the-emerald-buddha-or-wat-phra-kaew-royalty-free-image/1290084794); [Alamy example](https://www.alamy.com/thailand-bangkok-grand-palace-wat-phra-kaeo-the-galleries-wall-paintings-image151437309.html)
- **Commissioning**: US benchmark of $150–$350 per person for headshots — [BetterPic](https://www.betterpic.io/blog/company-headshot-photographer-pricing-2026). Bangkok-specific pricing was not found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Suggested sourcing mix**:
  1. Commission one half-day to one-day Bangkok shoot: partner portraits, the office, hands at work, and a set of material still lifes (paper, pens, ledgers, brass, teak). This becomes the signature library.
  2. Fill gaps from Stocksy or Adobe Premium, searching texture, architecture and still-life terms rather than "business".
  3. Unsplash+ for low-stakes article thumbnails only.
  4. Avoid free Unsplash for anything showing identifiable people.
- **Thai cultural caution** (opinion, unsourced; verify): avoid Buddha images, royal imagery and temple interiors as decorative backdrops for a commercial finance brand. In Thailand these are sensitive, and royal imagery has legal sensitivity. Abstract Thai architecture and materials are safer: shophouse façades, Sino-Portuguese details, teak, rattan, silk, celadon, lotus-pond textures.
- Keep a licence register: image ID, source, licence type, date, and any release information. This protects the firm if a claim arrives later.

### Gaps
- Bangkok commercial or corporate photographer day rates in THB: no reliable source found. Get quotes directly.
- Offset (Shutterstock's premium brand), Getty Creative licence details and Death to Stock pricing were not verified this session.
- Thai legal rules on commercial use of royal or religious imagery were not researched with sources.

## 7. Image accessibility: alt text norms

### Takeaway
Decorative images (textures, mood still lifes, background video) get empty alt (`alt=""`) or are hidden from assistive technology. Informative images (partner portraits, an office photo that conveys something) get short, specific alt text. Functional images (an image link) describe the destination, not the picture. Do not start with "image of".

### Cited Findings
- The W3C WAI "alt decision tree" is the canonical guide. Its rules (recalled, **not verified this session** because the fetch was blocked):
  - decorative or redundant images use `alt=""`
  - informative images give a concise equivalent
  - functional images describe the action or destination
  - complex images need a longer description nearby

  — [W3C WAI alt decision tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/)
- Background or ambient motion should respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — [specification.website](https://specification.website/spec/accessibility/reduced-motion/)

### Inferences
- **For the builder**:
  - Make alt text a required field, with a "decorative" checkbox that outputs `alt=""`.
  - Portraits: "Name, Position" is usually enough when the name already sits in a nearby caption; otherwise use alt="Khun X, Partner, Tax Advisory".
  - Video backgrounds get `aria-hidden="true"` when purely decorative.
  - Thai pages will need Thai alt text.
- Make sure text over imagery keeps WCAG contrast, especially oxblood on photos. Add a scrim or overlay in the brand colour.

### Gaps
- The W3C page could not be fetched, so its wording should be checked before it is quoted in a guideline.

# DA brand mark inspiration — research notes

For: DA Accounting & Consulting Co., Ltd. (Thailand; EN / TH / 简体中文)
Brief: luxury-house feel; oxblood `#7a2229` on warm paper `#f2eeea`; night `#1b1112`; brass `#7d5f28` / `#c4a06a` for marks; refined high-contrast serif (Cormorant Garamond). Signature details: double-rule frame, paper grain, monogram seal, stitched oxblood band, deliberate emptiness. Banned: illustrations, icon grids, rounded cards, gradients/glass, giant sans headlines.
Compiled: 2026-10-05.

## 1. Two-letter monograms and ciphers

### 1.1 Vocabulary and construction principles (the "rules")

- **Monogram vs cipher.** In a *monogram*, "one letter forms part of another and cannot be separated from the whole". A *cipher* "is merely an interlacing or placing together of two or more letters", where no letter depends on the others for its parts. Source: Turbayne & Carlton Studio, *Monograms & Ciphers* (1906), Project Gutenberg — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40023 (extract via https://gutenberg.org/files/40023/40023-h/40023-h.htm).
  → For DA: a **true monogram** means a shared stroke. A **cipher** means interlaced or adjacent letters. Both are legitimate. The shared-stroke version reads as more "designed" and more ownable.
- **Three forms** (same source):
  - *Imposed*: letters laid over one another.
  - *Extended*: letters side by side.
  - *Continuous*: one unbroken line.
- **Principal letter** (same source):
  - In the imposed form, "the principal letter should be accentuated, either by making it slightly larger, heavier…".
  - In the extended form, when filling a circular space, the principal letter may sit in the centre and be drawn larger.
- **Legibility test** (same source): the difficulty is to make a monogram "where all the letters will read as intended, and unintended letters are not suggested."
  → DA risks: an A inside a D can read as **Δ** (delta) or as a "play" triangle. A D bowl attached to an A can suggest **R**, **Æ** or **Ð**. Test against these.
- **Mirror symmetry only works for identical letters.**
  - Gucci GG: two G's mirrored, one forward and one reversed, "creating a symmetrical pattern that works as both a brand mark and a decorative motif" — https://logotyp.us/logo/gucci/
  - Fendi FF "Zucca": two interlocking F's, one inverted (Lagerfeld, 1965) — https://www.fashionphile.com/blogs/academy/iconic-fendi-logos
  - Erik Penser Bank: Bedow's EPB is a "symmetric ambigram" that reads the same upside down, made of "monolinear lines and loops" — https://www.typeroom.eu/content/erik-penser-bank
  → D and A are not mirror twins. DA must get its balance from **enclosure and axis alignment**, not from mirroring.
- **Face-to-face pair inside an oval.** Cartier's double C: two interlocking C's "arranged face-to-face within an oval frame". It debuted at the 1900 Paris World Exhibition, attributed to Louis Cartier — https://1000logos.net/cartier-logo/ (via brandkit.pro / 1000logos; **[unverified]** detail on the oval).
- **Mixing styles inside one mark.** Cassandre's YSL (1961) blends "serif and sans serif, roman and italic". The letters are "intertwined vertically, with the central 'S' framing the 'Y' and the 'L'". It was his single proposal, and it has been in use unchanged since 1963 — https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/18068/the-monogram-of-saint-laurent ; https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2179574
  → Lesson: one letter can **frame** the other. Here, the D's bowl frames the A.
- **The Dürer precedent: the closest historical model for a D+A mark.** Albrecht Dürer's AD monogram sets the **D inside the legs of a tall A**. It worked as a trademark and quality seal on his prints. In 1506 Dürer won a case against Marcantonio Raimondi: copies could continue, but not with the monogram — https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/blog/durer-or-not ; https://collection.kunsthaus.ch/en/collection/item/9015
  → DA can invert this: the **A nests inside the D**, or the D carries the A. This is a 500-year-old, art-historically respectable construction for exactly these two letters.
- **Royal ciphers** are prepared by the College of Arms, with the monarch choosing from designs.
  - Victoria's VR is interlaced. Elizabeth II's E II R uses separate letters under a crown.
  - Charles III returns to an interlaced C and R.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_cypher
  → Both "interlaced" and "separated under a device" are heritage-correct.

### 1.3 Banks, old firms, watchmakers

- **Coutts**:
  - 2011: identity started at Pentagram (John Rushworth), finished by Coley Porter Bell. It kept the three crowns, "slightly simplified" — https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/november-2011/coutts-rolls-out-new-identity/
  - 2020: FutureBrand added "vertical lines to symbolise a family tree" — https://www.futurebrand.com/news/a-new-era-for-private-banking-futurebrand-refresh-coutts-brand-positioning-and-visual-identity
  → **Simplify the heritage device; add rules (lines) as the modern layer.**
- **C. Hoare & Co.**: has traded "at the sign of the Golden Bottle" for 350+ years. The sign was a gilded leather bottle and served as the address before street numbers — https://hoaresbank.co.uk/our-history
  → One consistent sign, kept forever.
- **Pictet**:
  - The lion, adopted 1955, had the motto "Fais bien et laisse dire".
  - In 1974 the motto was replaced by **"1805"**, the founding year.
  - In 1997 the lion was "confined to a cage" next to PICTET; redrawn for digital in 2015.
  - Source: https://www.pictet.com/lu/en/about/the-pictet-logo
  → **Founding year as a typographic element.** Emblem **enclosed in a frame.**
- **Rothschild**: a fist holding five arrows (the five sons; Psalm 127); motto *Concordia, Integritas, Industria*; arms modified 1822 — https://rothschildarchive.org/family/the_rothschild_name_and_arms ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_family
  → A **three-word values motto** suits a seal rim.
- **Lombard Odier**: 360° rebrand in 2016 ("Rethink Everything"), led by a branding chief hired from luxury fashion — https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/case-study-rebranding-swiss-bank/reputation-matters/article/1455188
  → Swiss private banks borrow luxury-house people and methods. This validates the DA brief.
- **Erik Penser Bank (Bedow, 2016)**: monoline looped EPB ambigram. The same looped line drives the illustration library. Custom low-contrast Antiqua "Erik Text" with Íñigo López Vázquez — https://www.typeroom.eu/content/erik-penser-bank ; https://bpando.org/2016/06/02/branding-erik-penser-bank
  → **One line-logic generates the whole system.** Very relevant to code-drawn SVG.
- **Patek Philippe Calatrava cross**: trademarked 27 April 1887. It appears on the **winding crown, caseback and clasp** — https://www.patek.com/en/manufacture/a-story-of-independence/the-calatrava-cross
  → The mark lives **small, in fixed places**.
- **Geneva Seal (Poinçon de Genève)**: cantonal law of 6 Nov 1886. The hallmark depicts the arms of Geneva (eagle and key). It is engraved on the movement and, since 2011, on the case — https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-geneva-seal ; https://wiki.grail-watch.com/index.php/Geneva_Hallmark
  → The seal works as a **certification of finished work**.
- **British hallmark**: sponsor's mark (initials in a shield; "45 different shield shapes"), fineness, assay office, and a date letter in a shield that changes yearly — https://www.assayofficelondon.co.uk/hallmarking/uk-hallmarks ; https://www.thegoldsmiths.co.uk/hallmarking-stories/a-field-guide-to-hallmarking
  → A **strip of small cartouches** is a ready-made system for "signing" deliverables.

## 2. Wordmarks for professional firms (serif capitals)

- **Hermès**:
  - "HERMÈS PARIS" is a custom serif in the Memphis lineage (Memphis by Rudolf Wolf, 1929). The house publishes no name, so the attribution is a guess — https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=8812 ; https://logotyp.us/logo/hermes/
  - The grave accent is "integral to the mark". "PARIS" works as a locative second line.
  → DA's second line can be a **place or founding year**: "BANGKOK", or "EST. 20XX" in the Pictet "1805" style.
- **Celine (2018)**: accent removed "to enable simplified and more balanced proportions". Letter spacing **narrowed** for a tighter look. The 1960s archive was the reference — https://www.dezeen.com/2018/09/04/celine-drops-accent-hedi-slimane-logo-design/ ; https://hypebeast.com/2018/9/celine-new-logo-hedi-slimane
  → The main name is **tightly spaced**. Only the descriptor is spaced widely.
- **Burberry (2023)**: returned to a serif and the 1901 Equestrian Knight archive — https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/6-february-10-february-2023/burberry-new-logo-daniel-lee/ ; https://www.dezeen.com/2023/02/07/burberry-daniel-lee-logo-equestrian-knight-design/
  - This joins Bottega Veneta and Ferragamo in a serif revival — https://monocle.com/design/serif-revival-fashion-brand-logos/
  - A designer quoted there: sans-serif-only branding "led to a dry standardisation".
- **Aesop**: a humanist serif wordmark with "calligraphic warmth"; everything else is set in Suisse Int'l. "The restraint is the entire brand" — https://fontalternatives.com/inspiration/aesop-suisse/
  → **Serif only for the mark and display.** Restraint everywhere else.
- **Slaughter and May**: switched from "&" to "and" in 1956 — https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/playing-name-games/35539.article ; Northbank Design identity programme, 2001 — https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/28-june-2001/northbank-takes-case-for-slaughter-and-may/
  → The ampersand is an editorial choice. If DA keeps "&", make it a **feature**.
- **Bringhurst** (*Elements of Typographic Style*):
  - Guideline 5.1.3: "In heads and titles, use the best available ampersand". The italic "&" is often the more decorative one.
  - Capitals and small caps get letterspacing; lowercase does not (Goudy's "steal sheep" dictum).
  - Sources: https://retro.simplebits.com/posts/ampersands-2 ; https://caretypography.com/do-s-and-don-t-s-in-using-type
- **Cormorant Garamond**: by Christian Thalmann (Zurich), free via Google Fonts. The family includes **Small Caps**, **Upright Cursive** and **Unicase** styles, Light–Bold — https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Cormorant/about ; https://fontsource.org/fonts/cormorant-garamond/about
  → Real small caps for "ACCOUNTING & CONSULTING", and an Upright Cursive or Italic "&" are available in one free family.
- **Thai and Chinese companions** (for TH/ZH lockups):
  - Cadson Demak's **Taviraj** (serif Latin + looped Thai, thick/thin strokes "similar to the Latin", 9 weights) — https://fontsource.org/fonts/taviraj/about
  - **Maitree** (looped Thai, long Latin serifs) — https://rightfontapp.com/family/maitree
  - Loop-style Thai matches the high-contrast serif register — https://granshan.com/insights/thong-lor-and-the-return-of-the-loop-cadson-demaks-typographic-bridge-to-thai-identity
  - For Chinese, a Song/Ming serif (e.g., Noto Serif SC) is the natural partner **(design convention)**.

**"Co., Ltd." handling (design convention, informed by the above):** the Thai legal suffix must appear on the company seal (see §3). In the wordmark, keep it **out of the primary lockup**. Set it at the end of the descriptor line in small caps, the same size as the descriptor, with a thin space after each period ("CO.,&thinsp;LTD."). Alternatively put it only on the seal rim and in the legal footer. Never set it in full caps at name size.

## 3. Seals and hallmarks

### 3.1 Thai company seal (ตราประทับบริษัท)

- Content rules:
  - It must include the **company name in Thai**; English or another language may be added.
  - It must show the company-type suffix (Co., Ltd. / บริษัท … จำกัด).
  - A picture or symbol is optional.
  - Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/corporate-and-company-law/753806/company-stamp ; https://www.tilleke.com/?p=36741
- Thai law does **not force** a company to use a seal, but if one is registered, the company affidavit stipulates it. Signatures plus the seal then bind the company — https://www.ofm.co.th/blog/corporate-seal/ ; https://www.mondaq.com/corporate-and-company-law/753806/company-stamp
- **Shape is not prescribed** in any source found. The common round layout (Thai name around the top arc, English around the bottom, emblem in the centre) is observed practice, **not verified as a rule**.
- **Important caution:** the DA brand seal drawn for the website is a *brand device*. The firm's *registered* seal is a legal instrument filed with the DBD. Do not make the web seal look like a scan of the registered seal, and do not imply it is one. If the firm wants them to match, the owner should register the design deliberately.
- **Do not use the Garuda (ครุฑ)**: it is reserved for holders of a royal warrant ("โดยได้รับพระบรมราชานุญาต") — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_warrant_of_appointment_(Thailand) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Thailand

### 3.2 Corporate "common seal" (UK) and notarial embossers

- **UK common seal**: optional under the Companies Act 2006. If a company has one, its name must be "engraved in legible characters on the seal" — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/notes/division/5/12/7 ; https://www.informdirect.co.uk/tag/company-seal/
- It is usually an **embosser**: name around the rim, company number in the centre.
- **Notary seals (US states)**:
  - They require "a serrated (notched) or milled (ridged) border", in rectangular or circular form, with fixed text.
  - The embosser "crimps" paper into a raised impression.
  - Sources: https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/il-notary/journal-seal-fees/official-seal ; https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/1998/legislation/S1387
  → **A milled edge = "official".** Use it only on the emboss variant, and keep it fine.

### 3.3 Chinese seals

- **朱文 zhuwen** (red characters, yang): the characters print red. **白文 baiwen** (white characters, yin): the ground prints red and the characters stay white. Seals may combine both (朱白文相間印). Qin seals were mostly baiwen — https://www.ibiblio.org/chineseculture/contents/arts/p-arts-c01s04.html ; https://www.vmfa.museum/interactives/beyond-the-walls/seal/
- **Reading order**: right to left, top to bottom. A 2×2 seal is read starting top-right, down, then the left column — https://vincentpoon.com/the-seal.html
- **Craft**: seal-script calligraphy, idea and carving are the "three indispensable methodologies". Composition (章法) means balancing the strokes within the border — same source.
- **PRC official company seal (公章)**: circular and red, bearing the registered name. It can be made **only by PSB-designated makers** and is filed with the police — https://chinajusticeobserver.com/a/what-is-the-chinese-company-stamp-and-how-to-use-it ; https://translate.hicom-asia.com/area/official-red-seal/
  → **Do not imitate it** (no round red seal with a central star).
- **Foreign names**: usually transliterated by sound; a native speaker can help choose meaning — https://translate.hicom-asia.com/area/chops
- Choosing a Chinese brand name: the strongest names match both sound and meaning. China is "first-to-file", so register the name before using it — https://harris-sliwoski.com/chinalawblog/china-trademarks-brand-names-copycats-and-soundalikes/ ; https://www.registrationchina.com/articles/chinese-translated-company-name-trademark/

**Respectful use for DA (design convention, grounded in the above):**
1. Use a Chinese seal only if the firm has an **agreed Chinese name**, checked by a native speaker and ideally registered.
2. Make it **square**, never round with a star, so it reads as an artist's/name seal (闲章/名章), not a 公章.
3. Use **zhuwen** (oxblood lines on paper) so it matches the rest of the system.
4. Have the seal-script glyphs drawn by a qualified calligrapher or carver, or taken from a licensed 篆书 font. **Do not generate seal script by code.**

### 3.4 Wax seals and signets

- A seal matrix is cut **in intaglio**, so the impression comes out in **relief and mirror-reversed** — critical when text is involved. Signet rings carry the device and legend on the bezel or on an engraved gem — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_(emblem)
  → The "pressed" look comes from **raised relief + a reversed-matrix logic**. On screen, draw the impression (right-reading), not the matrix.

### 3.5 Security engraving (finance heritage)

- **Guilloche**: "thin lines interwoven according to the rules of geometry".
- **Microtext**: "very small text… repeating phrases, denomination values, or the name of the issuing authority".
- **Line intaglio**: line width grows with engraving depth.
- Sources: https://www.koguja.ee/en/articles/comparisons-and-analysis/microdetails-in-banknotes-what-collectors-miss ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08993 ; https://blog.fontlab.com/2026/04/14/money-lace-guilloche-intaglio-spirograph/ (title and extract only)
- **The maths is directly codeable.** A hypotrochoid is:
  - x = (R−r)cosθ + d·cos((R−r)θ/r)
  - y = (R−r)sinθ − d·sin((R−r)θ/r)
  - Banknote guilloche was historically drawn on geometric lathes, "mechanically identical to a Spirograph scaled up".
  - Source: https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/spirograph
- **Thai Series 17 notes**: "uniline repeating microtexts '20'… on the edge of guillochés surrounding Arabic numerals". There is also an ornamental colour-shifting patch with Thai patterns — https://www.bot.or.th/en/our-roles/banknotes/History-and-Series-of-Banknote-And-Commemorative/banknotes-series/banknote-series17/securitybn100polymer.html ; https://www.bookmyforex.com/currency-exchange/counterfeit-detection-guide/thai-baht/
  → **Microtext running along a guilloche edge, framing a numeral** is a precise, local precedent.

## 4. Code-made imagery that reads premium

- **Swiss 9th series (Manuela Pfrunder; 2016–2019)**:
  - Recurring core elements on every note: **a hand, a globe, a Swiss location and an object**.
  - Each note has a concept (time, light, wind, water, matter, language).
  - Globes are covered with isobars.
  - Microtext lists Switzerland's 4,000-m peaks.
  - Sources: https://www.coinworld.com/news/us-coins/swiss-national-bank-releases-final-issue-in-ninth-note-series ; https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/new-money_swiss-national-bank-unveils-latest-chf50-note/42071040 ; https://www.blog.alpian.com/trends/switzerland/time-light-wind-matter-heres-what-you-can-see-on-the-swiss-francs-in-your-pocket
  → **A fixed grammar of a few recurring elements**, with one concept per page. **Data-like lines** (isobars) used as ornament. **Microtext with real meaning.**
- **Norway (2017–2019)**:
  - Fronts by The Metric System and Terje Tønnessen: realistic maritime scenes.
  - Backs by Snøhetta: pixel/cube abstractions of the coast, "The Beauty of Boundaries".
  - Sources: https://www.digitaltrends.com/photography/blending-past-future-norway-gets-pixelated-new-series-banknotes/ ; https://designboom.com/?p=218394
  → Pair **one traditional side with one rule-based abstraction of the same subject**. The abstraction must come from real data (the coastline), not random noise.
- **Thai Series 17 (2018)**: portraits plus guilloche, microtext and Thai ornament as security elements; sizes and colours kept from Series 16 — https://siambusinessnews.com/9147 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banknotes_of_the_Thai_baht
- **Erik Penser Bank** (above): one monoline drives the logo *and* all illustrations. This is the best model for a code-drawn system.
- **Studio Dumbar / PTT (1989)**: an identity system built from the square as a building block, separated, rescaled and layered — https://eyemagazine.com/feature/article/flexible-geometry
  → A **kit of parts** with play inside strict rules.
- **Coutts 2020**: vertical lines for the "family tree" — https://www.futurebrand.com/news/a-new-era-for-private-banking-futurebrand-refresh-coutts-brand-positioning-and-visual-identity
  → **Rules as meaning**, not decoration.
- **Goyard / LV / Dior** (above): patterns derived from letters, used on surfaces such as bands and linings, never as the headline.
- **Accounting's own graphic language**: in financial statements a **single underline marks a subtotal and a double underline marks the final total** — https://community.board.com/discussion/18937/add-double-underline-in-board-double-underlining-is-required-for-final-subtotals-in-accounting
  → DA's **double-rule frame is literally the accountant's mark of a finished, verified total.** This is the firm's own "golden bottle".
- **Hermès saddle stitch (point sellier)**: two needles, one linen thread, crossing in the centre of the seam. The stitch **angle** is consistent and slanted, and a machine cannot reproduce it — https://www.permanentstyle.com/2011/09/hermes-how-leather-goods-are-made.html ; https://romestation.ca/blogs/news/saddle-stitch-vs-machine-stitch-what-sets-hermes-craft-apart
  → It specifies the "stitched band" exactly: **slanted dashes, constant angle, constant pitch**.

**How these keep it restrained (synthesis):**
- One generative motif per surface.
- Line-only, in one ink colour.
- The pattern encodes something true: a coastline, peaks, a letter.
- Pattern lives at the edges and in the background.
- Typography and emptiness carry the page.

## 5. Concrete design moves for DA (geometry-ready)

Units: cap height **H** of the serif D. Stem **S ≈ H/7**, hairline **h ≈ S/5** (Cormorant-like contrast). These ratios are a starting point **(design convention)**.

1. **"Dürer inverted" master monogram (imposed form).**
   - A full-height **D is the principal letter** (Turbayne: accentuate the principal letter).
   - An **A at ~0.62 H stands inside the D's counter**, its feet on the baseline.
   - The **A's crossbar is extended as a hairline** until it touches the D's stem on the left and the inside of the bowl on the right. One hairline ties the two letters together, which makes it a true monogram (shared stroke).
   - Precedent: Dürer's AD, the D inside the A (rmg.co.uk).
2. **Ligature variant (extended form).**
   - D and A sit side by side. The **D's bowl kisses the A's thin left leg at one tangent point**, with no overlap.
   - The A's crossbar continues left through the D as a hairline and on beyond both letters, becoming the **inner hairline of the double-rule frame**. The frame and the monogram are one drawing.
   - Model: Bedow's one-line system.
3. **Enclose in a vertical oval cartouche, Cartier style.**
   - Proportion 1 : 1.3. Outer rule = S (thick), inner rule = h, gap = 2h.
   - This doubles as the double-rule frame motif in miniature.
   - Tie-in: Pictet's "caged" lion, an emblem enclosed in a frame.
4. **Legibility gate.** Before approving any DA construction, test that it does not read as Δ, R, Æ, Ð or "AD" (Dunhill, Dürer) (Turbayne). The D must stay visibly first and larger.
5. **Accounting double underline as the frame's meaning.**
   - Every frame is a **thick rule over a hairline**, with the gap = 1.5–2 × thick.
   - Use the same pair as the "final total" rule under key figures. The frame says "verified total".
   - Source: the accounting double-underline convention.
6. **Wordmark lockup (three tiers).**
   - Tier 1: `DA` in Cormorant Garamond capitals, Medium/SemiBold, tracking 0 to −10 (Celine: tight main name).
   - Tier 2: `ACCOUNTING & CONSULTING` in true small caps, **tracked ~+150–200/1000**. The **"&" in italic or Upright Cursive** (Bringhurst 5.1.3).
   - Tier 3: `CO., LTD.` (or `BANGKOK · EST. YYYY`, Hermès "PARIS" / Pictet "1805" style) in small caps at tier-2 size. Hang it after a centred hairline 1/3 the width of tier 2.
7. **Seal (round, brand device, not the legal seal).**
   - Outer **milled edge** (notary convention) only on the "embossed" variant.
   - Double ring: thick + hairline.
   - Rim text: Thai name + บริษัท … จำกัด on the top arc, reading clockwise. English name + CO., LTD. on the bottom arc, reading left-to-right (counter-arc so it stays upright).
   - Two small lozenges at 3 and 9 o'clock separate the scripts.
   - The DA monogram (move 1) sits in the centre.
   - Content follows the Thai seal convention (Thai name required, suffix shown, emblem optional).
8. **Embossed (blind) rendering without gradients.**
   - Draw the seal twice in paper tones: a **1-px highlight offset (−0.5, −0.5) in a lighter paper** and a **1-px shadow offset (+0.5, +0.5) in a darker paper**. No fill, no blur, no gradient.
   - This reads as a relief impression (seal matrix → relief impression).
9. **Microtext rule.**
   - Replace the inner hairline of a frame with a `<textPath>` of repeated `DA ACCOUNTING & CONSULTING CO., LTD. · บริษัท … จำกัด · ` at 3–4 px small caps in brass. It reads as a line at arm's length and as text up close.
   - Precedent: Swiss peaks list; Thai Series 17 microtext on the guilloche edge.
   - Use one per page at most.
10. **Guilloche rosette in brass hairline.**
    - A hypotrochoid with integer R:r (e.g., R=96, r=36, d=52), 0.35 px stroke, `#c4a06a` on night or `#7d5f28` on paper.
    - Use it only **behind a single numeral or the seal centre**, and ring it with microtext (Thai Series 17 precedent). No fills and no colour ramps.
11. **Letter-derived pattern for the stitched band, Goyard style.**
    - Reduce the **A's apex to a chevron** (two hairlines at the A's leg angle) and tile it in offset rows at 6–8 px pitch.
    - Use it only inside the oxblood band, in brass at low ink coverage (Goyard: a pattern made from the "Y").
12. **Saddle-stitch band.**
    - The oxblood band is edged by a row of **slanted dashes**: length 3h–4h, angle 20–30°, pitch constant, in paper or brass colour.
    - Add a hairline "groove" 2h inside the stitches.
    - Precedent: Hermès point sellier, with a constant stitch angle.
13. **Hallmark strip for documents and PDFs.**
    - Four tiny cartouches in a row: sponsor's mark (DA in its oval), office mark (the seal), **date letter** (a year code in a changing cartouche shape), service mark (e.g., TAX / AUD / ADV).
    - Place it in the footer and on report covers (British hallmark structure; Patek's fixed small placements).
14. **Fixed placements, small.** The monogram appears only at a few fixed points (favicon, header-left, footer seal, PDF closing), always at the same size step (Calatrava cross on crown, caseback and clasp). Emptiness elsewhere.
15. **Chinese seal (only once a Chinese name is approved).**
    - Square, zhuwen (oxblood lines on paper), 2×2 characters read right column first.
    - Glyphs drawn by a calligrapher or taken from a licensed 篆书 font.
    - Placed small, beside the 简体 wordmark like an artist's seal. Never round with a star.

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Make one shared stroke (the A's crossbar hairline) the logic of the whole system: monogram, frame, rules.
- Keep the D dominant and first. Keep the main name tight and the descriptors widely tracked in small caps.
- Use an italic or upright-cursive "&" in titles only.
- Use line-only ornament in a single ink (brass on night, oxblood or brass on paper), and only at edges and backgrounds.
- Let ornament encode something true: the firm name in microtext, the double underline of a final total.
- Test every mark at 16 px (favicon) and at 600 px. Hairlines must survive at 1x (min 0.5 CSS px), or switch to a "small-size" master with thicker hairlines.
- Put Thai first on the seal, with the legal suffix shown.

**Don't**
- Don't mirror or rotate D and A for fake symmetry (that trick is for GG, FF, EPB).
- Don't let the monogram read as Δ, R, Æ, or "AD".
- Don't use the Garuda, a crown, or any royal or state emblem. Don't imitate the PRC round red 公章 with a star, or the firm's registered DBD seal.
- Don't generate Chinese seal script or Thai lettering by code-guessing. Use real fonts or a calligrapher, checked by native speakers.
- Don't use more than one guilloche or microtext element per view. No filled rosettes, gradients, glows or "generative noise".
- Don't put "Co., Ltd." in caps at name size. Don't letterspace lowercase.
- Don't use pictorial emblems (carriages, lions, bottles). For DA, the "sign" is typographic and geometric.
